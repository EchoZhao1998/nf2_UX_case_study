


# MediPort — Final Pitch
### 5-minute pitch script + 8-slide deck
---
## Pitch Strategy
**Target delivery:** ~4:15–4:30, leaving room for pauses and Q&A.
**Structure:**  
Problem → Evidence → Solution → Advantage → Feasibility & Business → Ask
**Core message:**
> **MediPort helps patients turn scattered medical records into something they can actually use in a consultation.**
---
# The Script# MediPort — Short Pitch Script

## Slide 1 — The Problem

Hi, I'm Echo.

I have NF2, a rare disease.
And I lost my hearing because of it.

Let me show you a problem I face at hospital visits.

I may have years of records,
from different hospitals,
in paper reports and MRI films.

At the same time,
I may be trying to read the doctor's lips.

So the problem is simple:

**My records are scattered.
But I have to connect them.**

---

## Slide 2 — Why It Matters

And this is not just my problem.

I surveyed 38 NF2 patients and caregivers.

**Zero had a tool to bring their records together.**

66% still use paper or films.

71% reported problems communicating with a new doctor.

So the problem is not just storing records.

It is **using them across hospitals.**

---

## Slide 3 — The Solution

That's why I'm building **MediPort**.

The idea is simple:

**Capture. Organise. Share.**

After a visit,
I take a photo of my records.

MediPort reads the key information
and builds one health timeline.

Before my next visit,
I can create a short summary for my doctor.

And each key result links back
to the original record.

---

## Slide 4 — The Value

The key change is this:

**I don't have to tell my whole story.
I can show it.**

The doctor can see my history
without rebuilding it from a conversation.

For me,
written information also makes communication easier.

**The record becomes part of the conversation.**

---

## Slide 5 — Why MediPort?

Why not use a cloud drive?

Because a cloud drive stores files.

Why not use a hospital app?

Because it may only show one hospital's records.

MediPort connects the information
across hospitals.

It is:

**Patient-owned.
Easy to follow.
Built for the consultation.**

---

## Slide 6 — Can We Build It?

Yes.

The technology already exists.

Camera.
OCR.
AI.
Timeline.
Summary.

My first version will focus on one thing:

**Turn messy records into a clear, trusted timeline.**

And MediPort does not diagnose.

It only helps organise the patient's information.

---

## Slide 7 — Business & Scale

I would start with NF2.

Then expand to other rare diseases
and people who use many hospitals.

The model could be:

**Free basic use,
with paid AI features and family sharing.**

There is also potential
to work with patient organisations and insurers.

So:

**NF2 is the starting point,
not the limit.**

---

## Slide 8 — The Ask

My next step is simple:

**Test MediPort in a real consultation.**

I want to work with:

Patients.
Clinicians.
Rare-disease groups.

And I am looking for
Monash's help with partners
and a pilot.

My goal is simple:

**Turn a workaround I built for myself
into a tool that can help many people.**

Thank you.

Design Rules

* 8 slides only.
* Do not use all 20 slides just because 20 is allowed.
* One idea per slide.
* One strong sentence rather than paragraphs.
* Use your own medical records and hi-fi prototype.
* Avoid stock photography.
* Minimum body text: 24pt
* Headline: 40–60pt
* Strong contrast.
* Keep the slides readable from the back of the room.
* Use the same visual language as your existing MediPort prototype.

Visual hierarchy

The strongest slides should be:

Slide 1 → Slide 3 → Slide 4 → Slide 8

These are:

Problem → Product → Payoff → Ask

⸻

Delivery Notes

1. Don’t aim for exactly five minutes

Aim for around:

4:15–4:30

This gives you room for:

* pauses
* audience reaction
* slight variation in speaking speed
* a clean ending

Do not try to fill every second.

⸻

2. Pause at two moments

After:

“Zero had a tool.”

Pause.

And after:

“I don’t have to narrate my medical history. I can show it.”

Pause.

These are your two strongest lines.

Let them land.

⸻

3. Don’t read the slides

Your slides should work like visual anchors.

For example, Slide 3 only says:

Collect first. Organise later.

You explain the three steps.

The judges look at the product.

That is much stronger than giving them a paragraph to read.

⸻

4. Accessibility during Q&A

After “Thank you”, if necessary:

“I lip-read, so during Q&A, please face me when you speak.”

That’s enough.

Don’t over-explain your accessibility needs.

⸻

Likely Q&A

1. How is this different from existing health-record apps?

Most of them work within one provider. MediPort is designed for the gap between providers, where the patient has to connect the information.

⸻

2. What if the AI gets something wrong?

The summary is traceable. Important values link back to the original record, so the patient or clinician can verify them.

If they push further:

For uncertain extraction, the system should ask the user to review it rather than silently present it as fact.

⸻

3. Is this a medical device?

The initial product is designed to organise and present the patient’s own information. It doesn’t diagnose or recommend treatment.

If they push further:

Regulatory requirements would still need to be assessed as the product develops.

Don’t say “we stay outside medical-device regulation.” That’s too absolute.

⸻

4. Why would people pay?

That’s something I still need to validate. My initial hypothesis is a low-cost subscription, with caregivers and organisations as potential payers.

Then stop.

Don’t try to prove willingness to pay before you’ve tested it.

⸻

5. Can you build this?

I’m building and validating the UX prototype. The MVP would need engineering support, which is part of what I’m looking for next.

This is a much stronger answer than pretending you already have the engineering resources.

⸻

The Three Things You Need to Prove

Don’t try to prove everything in five minutes.

The judges only need to leave the room believing three things:

1. The problem is real.

The patient is currently connecting fragmented healthcare systems.

2. MediPort directly changes the consultation.

I don’t narrate. I show.

3. You know what needs to happen next.

Test it with clinicians in a real five-minute consultation.

If those three things land, the rest of the pitch supports them.
